# Heuristic: Reachable Control

## Recognition

This heuristic fires when a workflow, state transition, resource rule or delegation arrangement threatens a person's practical ability to inspect, stop, redirect or communicate about work they are authorised to direct.

Cues:

- Busy, waiting or degraded work removes the permitted input or cancellation path.
- A capacity limit blocks new work and also removes the means to direct recovery.
- Background reports displace a question or response the person needs to read.
- A handoff changes the responsible destination before the person can find and address it.
- An unrelated save, read or refresh must succeed before the person can communicate a correction.

A path is usable only if the person can find it, understand its effect and reach the intended destination. Its mere presence does not establish those properties.

## Move

1. Identify the work, its responsible destination and the interventions the person is authorised to make. Preserve that authority boundary.
2. Separate admitting or executing work from accepting direction about it. A resource limit or busy state need not eliminate safe communication, inspection and recovery.
3. Keep the responsible destination and pending human dialogue reachable. Retain useful reports without letting routine activity displace the question or response needed to direct the work.
4. Check the meaningful action in the affected state, not merely the control's presence while idle. During handoff, check that the person can find and address the replacement before treating the transfer of control as complete.
5. If an action cannot take effect safely or the transport cannot deliver it, state that specific boundary and preserve the safe alternatives that remain. Distinguish received direction from completed action.

Use an existing adequate path. This heuristic does not require a second control surface, a central coordinator or continuous human supervision.

## Why This Works

Authority has little practical effect when the system's own work makes the direction path unusable. Preserving that path lets the person change priorities or respond to an unexpected state without first needing the blocked work to finish successfully.

The requirement concerns continued influence over the work, not exposure of every mechanical step. Automation can remain autonomous while the person retains a usable way to intervene.

## When NOT to Apply

- The requested intervention exceeds the person's authority or a binding restriction.
- The relevant path is already usable and the change does not threaten it.
- The work is complete and no continuing decision or recovery requires direction.
- The unavailable feature does not affect permitted influence over the work.

Legitimate resource and safety limits remain binding. An operation that cannot stop safely need not promise an immediate stop. An unavailable transport must not be represented by a cosmetic control. Preserve the remaining permitted path and make its effect clear.

## Relationship to Other Pillars

System Autonomy assigns deterministic mechanism to the system and judgment to the agent. Reachable Control preserves the person's practical ability to exercise authorised judgment while that mechanism runs.

Governing Context preserves the understanding needed for integration and dependent decisions. Reachable Control preserves the person's usable route to direct that continuation.

Verification Reach checks whether evidence establishes that the control performs the claimed action in the relevant state.

Burden Absorption removes the effort of mechanical waiting. Reachable Control prevents that wait from removing permitted influence.

Committed Contribution keeps claims open to correction. Reachable Control also covers stopping or redirecting work whose claims are sound but whose priority has changed.
