# Pattern: Experienced Increment

## Intent

Arrange delivery so seeing or using a concrete result can correct the work before costly dependent decisions accumulate. An increment is a coherent prototype, representative journey or release that exposes an assumption. Dependent work relies on that assumption for its next consequential decision.

## Problem

A completion report or a passing proxy can coexist with a poor experience. Later work may accumulate around an untested assumption, a concrete finding may not change the next decision, or the effort of discovering and reporting what changed may make useful feedback rare.

A local pass also need not settle the whole design. The arrangement must let observations change the work, not merely add a review to its end.

## Solution

The pattern connects these functions. One participant may perform several functions, and no staffing roster is required.

| Function | Contribution |
|---|---|
| Purpose and bounds | Preserve the current request, authority, restrictions and the distinction between independent and dependent work. |
| Makers | Develop the means and produce the increment. An implementation report is not itself experiential evidence. |
| Observable increment | Expose the consequential assumption in a coherent result, at the place and state where it matters. |
| Observation | Examine that result. The person supplies direct judgment when available. A stand-in uses representative real content under matched conditions and names remaining limits. |
| Choice | Repair, continue, reframe, narrow, hold or stop the act affected by the finding. |
| Exchange | Carry the result's identity, what to inspect, concrete findings and the resulting decision through a usable channel. |

Use the following sequence:

1. Name the experience and assumption on which the next consequential decision depends. State what would contradict the current direction.
2. Select the smallest coherent increment that exposes it. A complete journey or replacement prototype can be the right unit. Preserve necessary and binding checks, and price discretionary checks against actual consequence and reversibility.
3. Present the result and identify what changed. Do not make the observer reconstruct it from reports or implementation machinery.
4. Observe within stated limits. A maker's direct observation can be evidence. Add independent observation when consequences or blind spots warrant it. A stand-in's captures do not establish the person's physical-device experience or overall acceptance.
5. Consume an available finding before the next consequential act that depends on it. A concrete discrepancy changes the affected repair or selection. A rejected whole calls for Frame Abandonment, not more polishing inside it.
6. Bound work that proceeds without relevant observation by the cost of a wrong assumption and the protection available. Independent queued work may continue with its unobserved status explicit. If the observation path fails, narrow the increment or hold the dependent act rather than repeat the same failed attempt unchanged.

Do not call an unobserved result experientially accepted. When the person's judgment arrives, it governs claims about their acceptance. Other observers may still expose defects they did not report.

## Trade-offs and Failure Conditions

Small increments can optimise locally while the whole remains wrong. Use a representative whole when that is the meaningful unit, and let whole-design rejection overturn local passes.

Observation and coordination cost time. A fixed critic, roster or gate on every increment can obstruct the work. Combine functions and keep the exchange proportionate without weakening necessary checks.

A stand-in remains a bounded proxy, especially when it shares the maker's briefing. Do not substitute descriptions, simulated states or a convenient viewing condition for the experience at issue.

The pattern fails when findings are collected but not consumed, dependent work is relabelled independent, observation tooling becomes the project, or reports displace the person's ability to respond.

A cheap rollback does not undo disclosure, external effects or every state change. Use stronger protection or an isolated prototype where restoration cannot contain the consequence.

## When NOT to Apply

- The relevant acceptance is fully specified and testable without experiential judgment.
- The next act does not depend on such an assumption.
- An existing delivery arrangement already supplies this dependency.
- The required experience cannot be exposed safely. Hold the dependent act or narrow the claim rather than substitute an inadequate check.

## Relationship to Other Pillars

Creative Emergence develops possibilities from what the work and participants offer. This pattern makes observation consequential for subsequent delivery.

Verification Reach and Grounding Preflight bound evidence. Redundant Corroboration guides independent observation. Proof Burden and Failure Cost Calibration calibrate checks and protection. Governing Context preserves the purpose and integration frame. Frame Inspection and Frame Abandonment govern a rejected whole.

Epistemological Grounding and Triangulated Truth preserve firsthand evidence within its scope. A person's own experience and acceptance are not established by an implementation report or a stand-in's different experience. Those other observations still supply evidence about what they directly reached.

Reachable Control preserves the person's usable route to respond and redirect. This pattern governs how delivery uses the response without making every release wait for it.
