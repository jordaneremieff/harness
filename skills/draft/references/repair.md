# Repair

How to fix a draft after the meter, the operator or a self-review finds a tell. The governing rule: repair above the form and keep every claim. A new draft with the same move is not a repair.

## Route the flag by altitude

| Flag | Level | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| A named symbol, such as a dash or a semicolon | Symbol | Run steps 1 to 3 on the flagged symbols only. |
| "Sounds like AI", "reads like a press release" | Register | Find each paragraph's thesis and rebuild the paragraph from it. Symbol edits cannot clear this flag. |
| "Too long", "too detailed" | Scope | Cut whole units the reader is not owed. Cutting a paragraph beats thinning every sentence. |
| "Audit this for tells" | Requested analysis | The analysis is the deliverable. This is the only case that produces an audit report. |

## Six steps

1. Test for a legitimate twin. Does the form do work a plainer form cannot? If yes, keep it and give the functional reason in one line. If the operator flags the same use again, the operator's judgement wins. Restructure at once.
2. Name the move. Say what unearned work the form did: glue between two unlinked thoughts, padding one point into three, softening a firm conclusion, a grand verb hiding missing data.
3. Rebuild above the form, or cut. Rebuild the unit from its concrete claim with the relation in words (because, so, although) and the evidence. If the unit holds no defensible position, delete it.
4. Diff the claims against the prior version. Was a claim dropped? Was a definite statement diluted with a hedge? Did ownership move to a passive or an unnamed source? A weaker claim needs a one-line reason that names the thin evidence. With no reason, the original commitment stands.
5. Ship the repair, not the narration. Deliver the text. Do not say what you removed or that the rules were followed. A short factual note is allowed after the text, such as "one paragraph was cut".
6. Run the meter on the final text. A clean result is a floor. It does not show the move is gone.

## Claim diff table

| Claim change | Stated reason | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Weakened or hedged | Yes, with the thin evidence named | Accept |
| Weakened or hedged | No | Reject: the repair weakened the claim to dodge the flag |
| Moved to an outside source | Named, checkable source | Accept |
| Moved to "observers" or "experts" | None or vague | Reject |

## Laundering families

Banning one form moves the habit to its neighbour. Treat each row as one tell.

| Original | Usual substitutes |
|---|---|
| Em dash | Spaced hyphen, double hyphen, semicolon, colon label, bracketed aside, fragment |
| A stock adjective such as "pivotal" | "crucial", "vital", "foundational", "cornerstone", "bedrock", "load-bearing" |
| "Delve" | "explore", "examine", "dig into", "probe deeply" |
| "Tapestry" | "rich mosaic", "interconnected matrix", "vibrant fabric" |
| "Testament" | "compelling proof", "enduring evidence", "clear demonstration" |
| "Serves as" | "functions as", "operates as", "represents", "embodies" |
| Three-item list | Four items, a lettered list, comma run, "First, Second, Third" paragraphs |
| "Not only X but also Y" | "Instead of just X, it offers Y" |
| A hedge such as "might" | "appears to", "arguably", "I believe", "the truth lies in between" |
| A banned precision word such as "byte for byte" | "exactly", "identical", "precisely the same" |

## Positive rules work better than word bans

- Say the mechanism and the number.
- Name the check you ran and its result.
- Write the first sentence so it delivers the finding, the decision or the answer.
- Write for a reader who arrives cold and did not watch the work.
- Describe a designed state as expected behaviour, not as a failure.
- A token-free draft can still carry the move. Do not treat an empty meter result as acceptance.
