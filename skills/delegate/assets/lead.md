# Lead contract

## Purpose <!-- required -->
One sentence: why this lead exists and where it fits the larger goal.

## Objective <!-- required -->
The composed outcome the lead must return to the orchestrator.

## Operator authority <!-- required -->
The operator's words verbatim in quotes, with the source (session, date, or thread).

## Restrictions and exclusions <!-- required -->
What the lead and its workers must not do.

## Permitted edits and acts <!-- required -->
The paths, branches, and acts this lead and its workers may use.

## Skills to load
The skills the lead reads before its first dispatch, with the reason; name delegate when the lead runs its own team.

## Fan-out ownership <!-- required -->
Which parts the lead delegates, to which preset each part goes, and what the lead keeps.

## Result references <!-- required -->
Direct the lead to keep exact result references and await them natively, never to poll.

## Composed answer <!-- required -->
Direct the lead to return one answer that integrates every worker result, with gaps named.

## Acceptance evidence <!-- required -->
The checks or observable facts that prove the composed answer is complete.

## End condition <!-- required -->
When the lead stops: all workers resolved, the answer composed, blockers named exactly. Post to threads and make other tool calls first; the last message is the complete deliverable.

## Reply route <!-- required -->
The exact session identity that receives the composed answer.

## Thread ID
The collaboration thread the lead creates, runs, or joins.

## Provider or budget notes
Quota walls, substitution order, or budget limits for the lead and its workers.
