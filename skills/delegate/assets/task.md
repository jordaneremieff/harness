# Task contract

## Purpose <!-- required -->
One sentence: why this task exists and where it fits the larger goal.

## Objective <!-- required -->
The outcome the contributor must deliver, stated as an observable end state; it can span implementation, tests, and documentation.

## Operator authority <!-- required -->
The operator's words verbatim in quotes, with the source (session, date, or thread).

## Restrictions and exclusions <!-- required -->
What the contributor must not do: paths, acts, tools, or decisions reserved to others.

## Permitted edits and acts <!-- required -->
The paths, branches, and acts this task authorises: a coherent piece of work, disjoint from concurrent edits by other owners.

## Starting sources
Files, threads, notes, or URLs to read first, with why each matters.

## Skills to load
The skills the contributor reads before work, with the reason, such as dig for an evidence question; leave empty when this contract carries the whole method.

## Dependencies and other owners
Work this task depends on or must not touch, its owner, and the peers the contributor can contact about shared findings or a proposed change of ownership.

## Acceptance evidence <!-- required -->
The checks, outputs, or observable facts that prove the objective is met.

## Output format and length <!-- required -->
The shape and size of the final answer: one message, its sections, its length bound.

## End condition <!-- required -->
The state at which the contributor stops and reports, including the blocker form. Post to threads and make other tool calls first; the last message is the complete deliverable.

## Reply route <!-- required -->
The exact session identity that receives the answer, and any thread to update.

## Thread ID
An existing thread to join or read when it serves this assignment; otherwise omit this slot. Keep contribution within 256 characters and message within 8,000 characters; write longer content to a file and post its path.

## Provider or budget notes
Quota walls, substitution order, or budget limits the contributor must respect.
